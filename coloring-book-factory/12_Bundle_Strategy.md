# 12 — Bundle Strategy

> One home run beats two doubles — but a *catalog* of home runs, bundled, beats
> them all. Bundles are where this factory stops selling products and starts
> selling an **asset library**.

Single books acquire customers. Bundles raise average order value, reward repeat
buyers, and turn a pile of books into a *collection* worth more than its parts.

---

## The Bundle Ladder

Launch bundles as the catalog reaches each threshold. Each tier discounts the
per-book price to make "buy more" the obvious choice.

| Tier | Trigger | Contents | Positioning | Discount vs. à la carte |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Starter Bundle** | After Book #3 | Vols 1–3 of the Witch's Cottage Series | "The Cozy Cottage Starter Set" | ~25–30% |
| **Collector Bundle** | After Book #5 | Vols 1–5 | "The Witch's Cottage Collection" | ~35% |
| **Mega Bundle** | After Book #10 | Vols 1–10 | "The Grand Cottage Library — 500 Pages" | ~45–50% |
| **Seasonal Bundle** | When 3 seasonal vols exist | Autumn + Winter + Spring | "Cottage Through the Seasons" | ~30% |
| **Cross-Series Mega** | After 2 full series | Witch's Cottage + Moonlit Tea House | "The Ultimate Cozy Coloring Vault" | ~50% + |

The discount rises with tier size: the customer always feels rewarded for going
bigger, and our marginal cost per extra book in a bundle is ~zero (digital).

---

## Why Bundles Work Here

1. **Zero marginal cost.** Adding Vol. 2 to a bundle costs nothing — every bundle
   sale is almost pure margin uplift on AOV.
2. **Perceived value explodes.** "500 pages for the price of ~5 books" is an
   irresistible anchor; the Mega Bundle reframes every single-book price as cheap.
3. **Repeat-buyer reward.** Series-first design means buyers *want* the next book;
   bundles let them buy the whole world at once.
4. **Catalog as moat.** Competitors can copy one book. They can't easily replicate
   a deep, cohesive 10-volume world sold as a vault.

---

## Pricing Architecture (principles, not fixed numbers)

- **Single book:** the customer-acquisition price. Keep it accessible.
- **Bundle per-book price:** always lower than buying singly — visibly so.
- **Anchor with the biggest bundle.** Show the Mega Bundle first so smaller tiers
  look like deals (price anchoring).
- **Platform split:** run bundles on **both** Etsy and Payhip, but push the
  highest-value bundles on **Payhip** (lower fees, owned customer + email list).
- **Never discount below the dignity of the work.** Bundles reward volume; they
  don't signal "cheap." Quality is the brand.

## Bundle Listing Assets (reuse the per-book templates)

Each bundle needs its own Etsy + Payhip listing built from `10` and `11`:

- **Title:** lead with page count + savings — e.g. *"Witch Coloring Book Bundle |
  500 Pages | 10 Cozy Cottage Coloring Books for Adults | Printable PDF Mega Set."*
- **Hero image:** a grid of all included covers + a bold "500 PAGES / 10 BOOKS /
  SAVE 50%" badge.
- **Description:** "Everything in the Cozy Witch's Cottage world, in one
  download." List each volume's theme; restate the per-book savings.
- **Cross-sell:** point bundle buyers to Series #2 and seasonal editions.

## Future Catalog Strategy (beyond the first series)

The bundle model is the reason we design *series*, not one-offs. The long-term
catalog map:

```
Series 1: The Cozy Witch's Cottage      → its own ladder of bundles
Series 2: The Moonlit Tea House         → its own ladder of bundles
Series 3: (next Tier-S niche from 03)   → its own ladder of bundles
        ↓
Cross-Series "Cozy Coloring Vault"  ← combines all series (top of the funnel value)
        ↓
Subscription / "Coloring Club"      ← recurring revenue: new book every month
```

**Recurring-revenue horizon:** once 15–20 books exist, launch a **Coloring Club**
(a new cozy book delivered monthly, on Payhip's membership feature). That converts
the asset library into predictable monthly income — the ultimate endgame of a
factory.
